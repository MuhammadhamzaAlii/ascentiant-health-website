Home Health Billing Update

What home health agencies need to know about PDGM, authorization trends, and the 2026 reimbursement landscape.

Home health billing has never been simple — but the past few years have raised the bar. PDGM shifted reimbursement from volume to acuity and timing. Payers tightened authorization requirements. And agencies that still rely on manual tracking are finding it harder to keep up with the documentation and billing precision that home health demands.

Here is where the industry stands heading into 2026, and what billing and clinical leaders should be watching.

PDGM — Still the Center of Gravity

The Patient-Driven Groupings Model (PDGM) remains the framework for Medicare home health reimbursement. Payment depends on timely initiation of care, accurate OASIS assessments, comorbidity adjustments, and episode timing — not simply visit counts.

  • Low Utilization Payment Adjustment (LUPA): Episodes with too few visits still trigger significant payment reductions. Agencies must balance clinical needs against LUPA thresholds in care planning.
  • Comorbidity adjustments: Accurate ICD-10 coding on the OASIS and claim directly affects case-mix weight and reimbursement. Undercoding leaves money on the table; overcoding invites audit risk.
  • Timing: The 30-day period and visit distribution within the episode continue to influence payment outcomes. Late starts and front-loaded visit patterns require active management.

PDGM is not new anymore, but agencies still lose revenue to LUPA episodes, comorbidity coding gaps, and OASIS-to-claim inconsistencies. Billing and clinical teams need shared visibility — not separate spreadsheets.

Authorization Trends — More Scrutiny, More Denials

Commercial and managed Medicare plans are applying stricter authorization requirements for home health services. Prior auth denials and retroactive denials are increasing — especially for recertification episodes and higher-acuity patients.

  • Verify authorization requirements at referral intake, not after the SOC date
  • Document medical necessity with specificity that matches payer clinical criteria
  • Track authorization effective dates and visit limits across the episode
  • Appeal denials with clinical documentation that addresses the payer's stated reason — not generic templates

Authorization failures are among the most preventable revenue losses in home health. Front-end intake and clinical documentation quality are your best defenses.

2026 Outlook — What to Prepare For

While specific rate tables are finalized annually, several trends are shaping the 2026 home health landscape regardless of the final numbers.

  • Continued Medicare rate pressure: Agencies should model episode profitability under conservative reimbursement assumptions and focus on LUPA avoidance and coding accuracy.
  • Value-based purchasing (HHVBP): Quality reporting and outcome measures increasingly affect payment. Billing teams need to understand how quality scores interact with base episode rates.
  • Audit and review intensity: RAC, UPIC, and payer medical review activity remain elevated. OASIS accuracy, face-to-face documentation, and homebound status support are primary audit targets.
  • Workforce and operational efficiency: Agencies that cannot scale billing operations without adding proportional headcount will feel margin pressure fastest. Platform-based workflow management is becoming a competitive necessity.

Practical Takeaways for Billing Leaders

Home health revenue is won or lost at the intersection of clinical documentation, OASIS accuracy, authorization management, and disciplined billing follow-up. Agencies that treat these as connected workflows — not siloed departments — consistently outperform on cash collections and denial rates.

If your LUPA rate is climbing, authorization denials are growing, or aged A/R is outpacing new episode billing, it is worth a structured revenue review before 2026 rate changes take effect.
